## Conversation purpose
Get the REVVL 8 Pro's headphone output loud enough, without building anything, by walking the Android volume-escalation ladder and then finding an off-the-shelf booster. Along the way it settled a three-device working arrangement so the phone's audio paths would stop fighting each other.

## Key outcomes
- Ordered escalation produced and acted on: push media Volume Up during active playback (Android keeps media volume separate from ring/call), push Media to max in Settings > Sound & vibration > Volume, check Bluetooth media-volume-sync/absolute-volume options, then enable Developer options (tap Build number 7 times) > Networking > Disable absolute volume and reconnect.
- Don's own-APK idea was declined in favour of an existing app: Volume Booster GOODEV (com.goodev.volume.booster, 50M+ installs, 4.6 stars), with XBooster / Volume Booster - Sound Booster named as the alternate.
- Safe operating point set and accepted: system media at 100% with the booster around +10-20% maximum, because '+100%/+200% can introduce clipping and can hammer headphones or your ears'.
- Don tightened it further to his own ceiling: 'I don't boost it that high ... I don't go plus 20. I go plus 10.' The assistant confirmed +10% as the new baseline if it sounds clean there.
- Three-device audio separation accepted: iPhone for talking to the assistant, REVVL 8 Pro for music and headphones, PC for voice-to-text and work - 'No crosstalk, no fighting audio channels'.
- A hearing-safety caveat was given and left standing: some limiting on the phone is hearing protection, so do not defeat every limit and then run headphones at max continuously.

## Important facts
- The REVVL 8 Pro routes audio over Bluetooth or USB-C, not a 3.5 mm headphone jack; the device is referred to as 'Revel 8 Pro' in speech and 'REVVL 8 Pro' in the replies.
- Developer options path on this device is Settings > System > Developer options; the Settings search bar also jumps there if the System entry is missing.
- The suspected ceiling was diagnosed as the phone under-driving the headphones rather than a genuine hardware limit, which is why a small +10% boost resolved it.
- The volume-normalization / loudness-normalization / ReplayGain setting inside the music app is a separate cause of quiet output and was checked as part of the ladder.
- The iPhone/REVVL/PC split was described as better than trying to make one phone handle voice, music, Bluetooth routing and dictation simultaneously.
